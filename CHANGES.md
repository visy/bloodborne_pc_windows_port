# Bloodborne PC - Windows Native Port Improvements & Changes

This document details the optimizations, fixes, and refactoring applied to ensure stability, smooth frame pacing, low CPU utilization, and clear diagnostics on 4-core / 4-thread systems (such as the Intel Core i5-4590, 14 GB DDR3, GTX 1650 4 GB) while dynamically adapting to other hardware configurations (AMD GPUs, hybrid P/E CPUs, variable RAM/VRAM, and audio configurations). It also documents features and stability improvements ported from Linux upstream 0.3.


## 0.7.4 (unreleased)

- **Fixed: guests can fight more NPCs that turn hostile** (player report: "guests can't attack NPCs"; thanks to vladtepes22): when an NPC turned on the host, for example Eileen after the host attacks her, or Alfred in Cathedral Ward when his hostile state is set at a load, a guest could not lock on to or hurt that NPC. All the game's hostile switches now make such an NPC a foe the guest can hit too, not only some of them.
- **Fixed: guests no longer leave the host's Chalice Dungeon right after arriving** (thanks to vladtepes22): summoned guests could be sent home 1-3 seconds after they arrived in the host's Chalice Dungeon while other party members were still in other worlds. The game's second "member missing" check now waits for them as the first one already did.
- **Fixed: the party network report no longer says "port open" when guests cannot get in** (thanks to vladtepes22): behind your provider's shared NAT (CGNAT) or behind a second router, the router opens the party port only on itself and guests from the internet still cannot reach you, but the report said the port was open. It now says why guests can't reach you and what works instead (a VPN, another player hosting, a public IPv4 address from your provider). A guest who never connected also gets the likely causes listed, and repeated lines are logged once.
- **Fixed: starting the game yourself after a crash no longer presses Continue on the title screen** (thanks to vladtepes22): after a crash in a party, a game you started by hand went past the title on its own. Continue is now pressed only on run.bat's automatic restart or with `BB_PARTY_AUTOCONTINUE=1`; `BB_PARTY_AUTOCONTINUE=0` always leaves the title to you.

## 0.7.3 (Windows port 0.7.3, late player reports: chalice dungeons, Blood Echoes, saves, items, abductions, settings layout)

- **Fixed: a save made while you are a guest keeps your own world's state** (player report: save editors crashed on it until you hosted and saved again): in another player's world the game saved your character without the part that records your own world (which of its enemies are dead, its objects, its bloodstains), because joining a party never gave the game the copy of it that a normal summon makes. A save written in the host's world now keeps that part from your previous save, as one made at home has it, so save editors read it and quitting as a guest and continuing later finds your own world as you left it. Items, Echoes and progress from the host's world are kept as before. The log says so (`Saves: userdata0000 was written in another player's world ...`); `BB_SAVE_GUEST_WORLD_FIX=0` turns it off. Saves already made that way are fixed by your next save, or opened through *Export for save editor*.
- **Added: Export for save editor / Import from save editor** (player report: save editors crashed on a save last made as a guest in another world, until you hosted your own world and saved again). In another player's world the game saves your character without your own world's state (which enemies are dead, objects, bloodstains of your world), and save editors such as Noxde's Bloodborne save editor look for that part to find your position, so they failed or read and changed the wrong bytes. The launcher's *Party* tab now has two buttons. *Export for save editor*: pick a character (single-player or party save, any slot) and a folder; you get a copy the editor can open (your save is not changed), plus a small `bbport_export.json` and, for a party save, the party's files for it. *Import from save editor*: pick the edited file; it is checked (a valid save, the slot it came from), you are asked first when the game saved that slot since the export or the name changed, your saves are copied to `user\save_backups` (`...-before-editor-import`), and what the export added for the editor is taken out again, so the save is as the game wrote it plus your edits and your party progress stays. Your save is only read by the export.
- **Fixed: early items for every player** (player report): three items from the start of the game reached only one player of a party. (1) **Starting weapons**: when one player took its weapon and sidearm from the messengers in the Hunter's Dream, the other player's messengers were gone for good, so it could never get its own (most often with *Party starts* set to start at once instead of after the prologue). Each player's pick is now its own: your messengers stay until you have picked yourself, in your own Hunter's Dream, and a save that lost its messengers this way gets them back at its next visit to the Dream. A guest who never took its weapons picks them in its own Dream (after *Leave*, or before joining). (2) **Iosefka's Blood Vial**: only the player who talked to Iosefka through the clinic door got one, and talking to her again did not help the others. Now whoever talks to her, every player gets a vial (once). (3) **Old Hunter Bell**: a guest staying in the host's Hunter's Dream never got it, and a guest's talk to the messenger gave it to the host instead. It now goes into every player's inventory once the host has it or any player gets it in the host's world.
- **Added: anyone can be abducted**: a new host setting, *Anyone can be abducted* (*System → Party rules → Deaths*, the launcher's *Party Rules* tab, `bbport.ini` `party_any_kidnap`), on by default. When a Snatcher grabs a guest in the host's world, the whole party is now taken to the Hypogean Gaol in Yahar'gul, just as when the host is grabbed: one loading screen for everyone, and you all wake up in the cell together. The grabbed guest's Blood Echoes stay in a bloodstain where it fell, as the host's do when it is grabbed. Only the first kidnap of the host's world does this, as in the game; later grabs are ordinary deaths. Off: only a grabbed host takes the party there, a grabbed guest just dies. The kidnap is never written into a guest's own save, so it cannot send the guest to the Gaol later in its own world.
- **Changed: everyone sees the Gaol's waking scene**: when the party wakes in the Hypogean Gaol after a kidnap (whoever was grabbed), every member now watches the waking scene, not only the host. The guests see it as soon as they are in the cell (their loading screen waits through the host's copy), and everyone gets the scene's +1 Insight as before. This replaces 0.7.1's "guests skip the Gaol's waking scene".
- **Changed: the party settings are sorted into the host's rules and your own**: the launcher's *Party* tab now only has hosting, joining, your name, the party code, password and the connection. Two new tabs hold the rest: *Party Rules* (what the host decides for the whole party: enemies staying dead, gathering, NPCs, covenants, travel votes, NPC hunters, friendly fire, shared lamps, the host's deaths, rot; they apply when you host, and guests follow the host's) and *My Party* (what each player picks for itself: party HUD, name tags, pings, the glow, join / leave notices, story sync, what you get as a guest, party saves). The *Rules & sync* window is gone. The game's *System* menu is sorted the same way: *Party rules: World*, *PvP & lamps*, *Deaths*, *Rot* and *Enemies* (guests see the host's values there) and *My party: Deaths*, *World*, *Story*, *Rewards*, *Display* and *Look & notices*; *Find via public DHT* moved to *Party network*. Your settings stay as they were.
- **Fixed: Blood Echoes are no longer paid twice when an enemy takes them**: when you died as a guest and an enemy picked up your Blood Echoes (the purple glowing eyes), killing it gave them back, and then the bloodstain the marker pointed to gave them a second time. Now it works as in the original game: while an enemy has your echoes there is nothing to pick up on the ground (with *Bloodstain marker* on, the marker follows that enemy), killing it gives them back once and the bloodstain is gone. Picking up the bloodstain first also leaves nothing for the enemy to give. Dying again before you get them back loses the old echoes, and the enemy that held them stops glowing. If the enemy is gone without paying (it unloaded far away), the bloodstain is there to pick up again after a few seconds. `BB_PARTY_STAIN_HOLD=0` turns this off.
- **Fixed: a guest who falls to death in a Chalice Dungeon gets up beside the party, not in the air above the dungeon** (player report): a guest who died from a fall in the host's Chalice Dungeon (for example off a lift) was put back at a spot taken from the game's template of the dungeon's lamp, not where that lamp really stands in the generated dungeon, so it appeared high above the dungeon and died from the fall again, over and over. A guest who dies from a fall in a Chalice Dungeon now gets up beside the nearest living party member, without a loading screen; its bloodstain stays where it last stood on safe ground.
- **Fixed: Chalice Dungeon lifts count as lifts in a party** (player report: "I died on the elevator"): the party's lift checks only knew the lifts of the main game and the DLC, so a ride on a Chalice Dungeon lift could be taken for a fall, and nothing caught a player who slipped through the moving car. The dungeon's lift cars are now recognised as soon as they move: riding one is never a fall, and a player who drops through the moving car is put back on it, as on the other lifts.
- **Fixed: guests can use the lamps in a Chalice Dungeon** (player report): a guest could open a dungeon lamp and choose *Return to the Hunter's Dream*, but nothing happened. It now works as at the lamps outside: the guest is asked whether to travel with the party or alone.
- **Fixed: guests can no longer change the host's enemy scaling** (player report): on a guest, the six enemy scaling sliders (*System → Party rules: Enemies*: boss and enemy health, damage, poise) could still be moved, and so could the rot switches in the overlay's Party tab. It was mostly cosmetic: once the host's rules arrived the game used the host's values (only in the first seconds after joining could the guest's own scaling apply), but the guest saw its own numbers and its own `bbport.ini` was changed. While you are a guest these rows are now read-only lines that show the host's values ("The host's setting: ..."), like the other host rules. Your own values are kept for when you host. The host also sends its rules as soon as a guest connects (before, a new guest could wait up to 15 seconds for them). In the launcher, the host's rules are on the new *Party Rules* tab, which says they apply when you host. The party reward rows (*Full rewards for guests*, *Host's item rewards*, Insight, key items) are each player's own settings, as before.
- **Fixed: *Gather the party* no longer drops a guest into the air** (player report, Old Yharnam): the host gathered while holding still half way up a ladder, and the guest was put beside it in the open ladder shaft and fell to the floor below (from higher up that is a fall death). The host now cannot gather while on a ladder or in mid-air (jumping or falling): it is told *Gather the party: not on a ladder or in the air*. If the host climbs or drops after asking, a guest who says *Yes* goes beside the spot the host asked from instead of beside the host in the air. And should a guest still find no ground where it was put, it goes back to where it stood and comes with one ordinary loading screen.
- **Fixed: a possible GPU crash ("device lost") from a broken particle effect**: a guest arriving in Cathedral Ward lost the game to a GPU crash in a particle draw. Neither of that effect's shaders can loop, so the likely cause is a draw count that was garbage, the same kind of problem that crashed the particle simulations earlier. Before each such draw, a tiny check on the GPU now looks at its vertex and instance counts. A draw that asks for more than 16 million vertices is skipped and logged (`Indirect draws: N skipped ...`), and the game goes on. In our tests the largest real draw of this kind had 45 vertices, and the check made no measurable difference to the frame rate. If the GPU still crashes, the crash report (`out\device_lost_*.log`) now also shows the counts the GPU read for that draw. `BB_INDIRECT_GUARD=0` turns the check off.
- **Fixed: a guest's messages and signs in the host's Chalice Dungeon belong to that dungeon**: a guest summoned into the host's Chalice Dungeon while its own ritual dungeon was selected at its altar still counted as standing in its own dungeon, so the messages and summon signs it left or saw there were those of its own dungeon, not of the dungeon it was in. In the host's dungeon the guest's game now counts the host's dungeon as the one it is in; back home, its own dungeon is its own again, and walking into it from the altar works as before. The guest's own dungeons in its save are never changed.
- **Changed: covenant outcomes are each player's own**: how the covenant quests end now stays in the world and save of the player it happened to. When Alfred has killed Queen Annalise in the host's world (ending the Vilebloods there), or the host revived her with the Queenly Flesh, or killed Valtr, a guest's own save keeps its own Annalise, Alfred and Valtr: joining the host's game no longer ends a covenant in your world, and nothing a guest does with these characters in the host's world reaches the host's save. While you are in the host's world you see the host's state, as before. Lamps, bosses, doors, shortcuts and the other NPC quests are still shared both ways. A host setting, on by default: *Covenants per player* on *System → Party rules: World*, the launcher's *Party Rules* tab (*Covenant outcomes per player*), `bbport.ini` `party_covenant_per_player`; guests see the host's choice read-only.
- **Fixed: a guest who joins the League in the host's world gets the Impurity rune**: talking to Valtr as a guest and joining the League gave the guest nothing (the rune is handed over by an event that only runs in your own world), and could give the host a rune instead. The guest now gets the Impurity rune in its own inventory and save, once, as with other NPC gifts; the host gets nothing for the guest's choice. The same goes for the League Staff Valtr hands over.
- **Fixed: a guest leaving the Hunter's Dream alone no longer resets the host's world**: when a guest left the Dream on its own by a headstone (*Travel with the party?* → *No*), the host got a loading screen and its world reset as if the guest had rested. Only the host's own rest resets the world now; the guest's trip is a plain trip for the guest.
- **Added: the party can vote on travel**: a new host setting, *Party votes on travel* (*System → Party rules: World*, the launcher's *Party Rules* tab, `bbport.ini` `party_travel_vote`), off by default. With it on, a party trip from a lamp, a headstone or the Hunter's Mark only happens when everyone agrees: after *Yes* to "Travel with the party?" every other player gets the game's *Yes* / *No* question saying who wants to go where. One *No*, or no answer within 20 seconds, cancels the trip for everyone, and the notice names who said no or didn't answer. When a guest starts a party trip, the host and the other guests vote. Travelling alone is never voted on. Guests see the host's choice on their *Party rules: World* page.
- **Fixed: the party notices and the party panel no longer cover the boss's health bar**: in a boss fight, the notice in the bottom left corner ("Spectating Hunter1 - Cross: next hunter", "Joiners wait: you are in a boss fight" and the like) and the party panel with your companions' names and health could sit on top of the boss's name and health bar. While a boss bar is up, a notice that would touch it now shows just above it, and the party panel moves up above it when it would cover it (in a small window, on a 16:10 screen or with three companions). The space above the bar is kept free for every bar when a fight has several bosses. On a 21:9 screen the panel keeps its corner: the boss bar is in the middle there.
- **Fixed: no more flicker and FPS drop when entering big rooms** (player report): walking into the Research Hall's main hall, or stepping out of a lift, could flicker and drop the frame rate for about half a second, mostly on cards with 8 GB or less and with larger graphics heaps. The game threw out hundreds of textures at once well before video memory was actually full, and had to load them all again in the next frames; it could also lose the picture of some effects it had drawn itself. Textures now only make room when video memory is really getting full (measured from what the graphics driver says is free for the game), go a few at a time, oldest first, never ones used in the last moments, and effects the game drew are kept. In our test with the same tight memory limit, the main-hall and lift moments went from several 50 ms frames to none.
- **Changed: crash logs say more about why the game stopped** (from Mrsuss60's Windows port): a crash log (`out\crash_<time>.log`) now lists every time the game could not get memory, the game's own error checks that failed before the crash (its "panic" messages with their source file and line, and its debug exceptions), and the instruction that crashed. The first few of these also go into the game's log as they happen. The game goes on after its own checks as it did before, as on a PS4.
- **Changed: crash exit codes by cause**: a crash ends the game with code 140 when the game read through an empty pointer (often right after memory ran out, see the crash log), 141 when the game stopped itself on a failed check, 139 for other crashes and 132 for an illegal instruction (the same code when the crash handler itself gets stuck). Bug reports can tell crashes apart by the code.
- **Fixed: a crash with a bad thread pointer no longer freezes the game**: a crash in code that reads the game's thread data could retry forever at full CPU instead of writing a crash log and closing; it now writes the log and closes. The game's start also keeps its own exit routine safe from being overwritten (from Mrsuss60's Windows port).
- **Changed: a much smaller `bbport.exe`**: the release `bbport.exe` no longer carries its debug information (about 100 MB less to download and unpack; from Mrsuss60's Windows port). Nothing changes in the game.
- **Added: NPC hunters fight the whole party**: when a story hunter turns on the host in a party (Alfred in Cathedral Ward or Cainhurst, the blood-drunk Eileen at the Grand Cathedral, Valtr, the hunters of the Hunter's Nightmare and the Research Hall), it now turns on every guest too: before, a guest still saw a friendly hunter it could not lock on to or hurt while it fought the host. Brador and the Fishing Hamlet's hunters are meant to show up on every guest's screen too when they come for the host (not tried in a real session yet). Everyone sees the same hunter, every guest can lock on to it and hurt it, and its death and the quest step after it count for the whole party, in every player's own save (Alfred's quest states are covenant outcomes and stay each player's own). Fights where an NPC helps you (Eileen against Henryk at the Tomb of Oedon) already worked for everyone and still do. Covenant outcomes stay your own: the host's Alfred killing Annalise is not written into a guest's save. A host setting, on by default: *System → Party rules: World → NPC hunters fight all*, the launcher's *Party Rules* tab (`bbport.ini` `party_npc_fights`).

## 0.7.2 (Windows port 0.7.2, the 0.7.1 known issues fixed, lamps, rot and look)

- **Fixed: the Research Hall lifts no longer leave without you**: on the two lifts inside the Research Hall (Old Hunters DLC), the car could leave again the moment you stepped off it, with you still at its edge or about to step back in, and you fell into the pit under it and died. The car now waits until its ride is fully over, as every other lift does.
- **Fixed: Laurence's arena no longer starts a boss fight too early or shuts a guest in**: before Laurence's fight, which only starts when the host brings Laurence's Skull to the altar, a guest going through his fog counted as starting the fight and was then stuck behind the fog until using a Hunter's Mark. Now his room is open to the whole party until the fight really starts, as it is for the host. Ebrietas's and Lady Maria's rooms, whose fights also start with something the host does there, work the same way. A guest at such a fog is told what starts the fight.
- **Fixed: the fog at the back of Ludwig's arena goes away when he dies**: with a guest in the world, the fog wall at the far door of Ludwig's arena stayed after his death, blocking the way on into the DLC until the guest left or the game was restarted. It is now gone in a party (and with *Open world* on it never comes up).
- **Changed: no "Waiting" notice over a cutscene you watch with the host**: while you watch the host's cutscene together with it, the notice "Waiting: the host is watching a cutscene" no longer appears over the scene. It still shows if the host's cutscene goes on after yours has ended, and when you are not watching the scene with the host.
- **Fixed: a guest right behind the host sees the host's cutscene with it**: at the door into Cathedral Ward, a guest standing a few steps behind the host did not see the host's cutscene. The guest was still on the other side of the map border, so the guest's game counted it as "elsewhere" and skipped the live scene. The guest saw the host frozen with "Waiting: the host is watching a cutscene" and got the scene later, on its own, after walking through. Now a guest in the same part of the world sees the scene together with the host when the scene's area is loaded on its game or it stands near the host (within about 30 m). A guest far away or in another area still gets no scene over the wrong scenery.
- **Fixed: a guest beside the host sees the host's boss intro at once, and only once**: when the host started a boss's intro cutscene (Father Gascoigne's at the Tomb of Oedon gate, for example) with a guest standing right next to it, the guest's game could start its own copy of the scene at the same moment and drop the host's. That own copy then took up to about 20 seconds to show: the guest saw the host standing frozen at the gate with "Waiting: the host is watching a cutscene", walked on, and got the scene later on its own. Now the guest's game waits for the host's scene and plays it together with the host. Only if the host's scene does not reach the guest within a few seconds does the guest's own copy play, and either way the scene plays once.
- **Fixed: a guest left in the Hunter's Dream can use the headstones again** (player report): when the host left the Dream alone and the guest stayed, none of the headstones (or the Doll, the workbench and other prompts) reacted for the guest, so it was stuck in the Dream. The guest's game had started its "the host is gone, going home" wait, which turns off every prompt until the next loading screen, and the party keeps the guest there instead of sending it home, so the wait never ended. It is now cleared as soon as the party keeps the guest: the headstones work and ask *Travel with the party?* as usual. *NO* takes you to that lamp on your own; *YES* brings the party together there (if the host already stands in that part of the world, you go there yourself and join it, without moving the host). Choosing the same place the host went to works too (before, that one entry did nothing for the guest until its next loading screen). The same wait also blocked lamps and item pickups for a guest left anywhere else when the host travelled alone.
- **Fixed: the camera follows you again after a deadly fall in a party**: when you died from a fall in a party (for example off a ledge in Old Yharnam) and got up again at a lamp without a loading screen, the camera could stay behind at the fall: it pointed at the sky and you never saw your own hunter again, even after the host died. Getting up after a fall now turns the game's falling-death camera off and puts the camera back behind you, as after the normal death loading screen. The same goes for the host, and for the rescue that puts a falling hunter back on safe ground. The camera also comes back level behind you instead of looking straight down.
- **Fixed: walking normally after dying on a ladder in a party**: dying while climbing a ladder and getting up again at a lamp without a loading screen left your hunter thinking it was still on the ladder, so you could only walk forward. Getting up now always takes you off the ladder.
- **Fixed: joining from another area no longer lights a lamp in the host's world**: a guest joining the host from another area arrived at the lamp nearest the host even when the host had not lit it, which lit that lamp in the host's world, and the host then got up there after its next death. A guest now arrives at a lamp the host has lit (the host's own last lamp first), or beside the host when there is none, and a lamp only counts as lit by a guest when the guest really lights it. A lamp lit by someone else never becomes your place to get up after a death.
- **Fixed: corpses and other objects no longer pop in and out as you move**: with *Model detail* set to *Highest*, the game drew many objects only while you stood within about 10-15 m of them; further away they vanished instead of switching to their simpler model, so the corpses in Central Yharnam's streets kept appearing and disappearing as you walked back and forth. *Highest* is no longer offered, in the game's *Display* menu, the overlay and the launcher; a setting saved as *Highest* now uses the game's own detail, which draws these objects at every distance. Render resolution and the upscaler never changed this. Far buildings still switch between detail levels as on the PS4.
- **Changed: rot is off by default**: rot is now off by default, also if your settings had it on from an earlier version (the game saved it as on without you choosing it). Turn it back on in *System → Rot* (a new page) or the launcher's *Party Rules & Sync* if you want it; that choice then stays.
- **Added: killing a boss cleanses rot**: when a boss dies, every party member in the host's world loses all their rot, alive or dead in that fight. A notice says "Rot cleansed: boss defeated". On by default (only matters with rot on).
- **Added: kill your killer to cleanse its rot**: when the enemy that killed you dies, whoever in the party kills it, you lose the rot that death gave you ("Rot -1: your killer is dead"). A fall or a trap has no killer to hunt, and a rest or a party wipe brings the killer back, so the chance is gone then. On by default.
- **Added: retrieving your Blood Echoes cleanses its rot**: picking up your bloodstain removes the rot of the death that dropped it ("Rot -1: Blood Echoes retrieved"), unless killing your killer already removed it. Dying again before you get there loses the stain and keeps that rot. On by default.
- **Changed: the shader cache knows which version made it**: unzipping a new version over an old one keeps the old shader cache, and a new version whose shader translation changed could load shaders the old version had prepared (wrong shaders, or a crash on start in the worst case). The cache now remembers which shader translation built it; when that changed, the game prepares its shaders again once (the "Preparing shaders" screen at start, a few minutes) instead of using the old ones. Moving from 0.7.1 to this version keeps your cache: the shader translation is the same.
- **Added: Boot freeze dump** (launcher *Advanced / Debug > Diagnostics*, off by default): if the game stays on a gray or black screen at start, it writes what every game thread is doing to `out\boot_hang_<time>.log` and the log after 20 s, for a bug report.
- **Fixed: guests can light lamps in the host's world again**: since 0.7.1 a guest walking up to a lamp the host had not lit yet got no *Light lamp* prompt, because the setting that hides NPCs the host's world no longer has (*NPCs as in host's world*) also hid the lamp itself on the guest's game. It now only ever hides people, never a lamp, a headstone, a messenger or another object.
- **Changed: a guest's own lamps no longer light up in the host's world**: joining a friend's world with a high-level hunter lit every lamp of that hunter's own world there, even lamps past bosses the friend had not beaten. Now the host's world keeps its own lamps. Anyone in the party can still light a lamp in the host's world: it is saved in the host's world and in every player's own save, and the host's lamps still reach each guest's save. The old behaviour is a host setting, *Guests bring their lamps* (off by default): *System > World & PvP*, the overlay's Party tab, the launcher (`bbport.ini` `party_guests_bring_lamps`). Guests see the host's choice read-only.
- **Fixed: the Lecture Building floor is drawn again**: with *Model detail* set to *Lowest*, the game leaves some models out entirely, the Lecture Building's floor among them, so you saw a flat beige surface (the background) where the wooden floor should be. Other parts of the world could go missing the same way. *Lowest* is no longer offered, in the game's *Display* menu, the overlay and the launcher; a setting saved as *Lowest* now uses *Lower*, which draws the whole world. With any other model detail setting the floor was always fine. (The 0.7.1 notes listed this as an issue every player sees: it was only in our test games, which ran at *Lowest*.)
- **Fixed: a boss no longer restarts its fight at the lamp after a party wipe**: after the whole party died near a boss's fog gate (Father Gascoigne's, for example), a guest's game could still hold the lost fight and start it again by itself a few seconds after the respawn, without anyone going through the fog, and the boss attacked the party at the lamp. Now after a wipe (or any other respawn) every player's game has the boss back at its start, as after a death in the normal game, and the fight only starts when someone walks through the fog.
- **Fixed: a lift no longer carries you back and forth in a party**: on the Nightmare of Mensis library lift and the lift at Cainhurst Castle, a hunter who stayed in the car when it arrived was taken straight back the other way, again and again, as long as anyone else in the party stood outside the car. These two lifts now wait, like every other lift, until everybody has stepped out before they count the ride as over.
- **Fixed: a lift ends in the same place for everyone**: after a ride on the Nightmare of Mensis library lift, a guest's game could keep the car at the other end until it rode again, so the lift then did nothing for that guest or rode from the wrong end. The guest's lift now always ends where the car really is, the same as the host's.
- **Fixed: Cainhurst Castle's lift stays in step for the whole party**: if the host's game did not follow a guest's ride on this lift (for example while the host was still watching the castle's arrival scene), the host's car stayed at the other end. The guest's ride now always moves the car in the host's world too, as soon as the host is not standing in it.
- **Fixed: every lift ride reaches the host's world**: when the host was far away, a guest's ride to a lift end it had already reached once (up, down, up again) never reached the host's world, so the host found the car at the other end when it came back. Every ride counts now.
- **Fixed: no lift ride is taken for a fall**: the fall rescue (a hunter falling out of the world is put back on safe ground) could still take a long ride on a lift it did not know, such as a Chalice Dungeon lift, for a fall and pull the rider out of the car partway down. It now only acts on a real fall, faster than any lift moves.
- **Changed: party notices are centred**: the text of a party message box (for example "The host is in a Chalice Dungeon: Party > Rejoin to go back") was drawn from the left edge of the box; every line is now centred in it, and a long message wraps between words so each line stays centred inside the box. ## 0.6.4 (Windows port 0.6.4, audio fix)

- **Fixed: crackling and popping sound on 4-core CPUs** (in the world and the pause menu, the title screen was clean): on CPUs with 4 cores or fewer the game's sound mixer thread ran below every normal game thread, and the sound reached the device up to 140 ms late. - **Changed: the sound cushion adapts to the PC**: it starts at two device periods (about 20 ms with Windows audio; it was about 11 ms, less than one period). Each time the device runs dry it grows by half (up to 200 ms), and it shrinks back by 5 ms per 20 s without a dropout, so a fast PC keeps the low latency and only a PC that needs it gets more. The game log says when it grows. `BB_AUDIO_CUSHION_MS` (10-200) fixes it instead. A queue that runs 200 ms over drops one buffer instead of being flushed.
- **Fixed (for testers): `BB_AUDIO_STATS` now counts starved buffers**: the queue was read after the new buffer was added, so "starved" was always 0; it now reads before, and the line shows the queue in ms, the cushion and dropped buffers. The log also lists each FMOD thread's Windows priority.
- **Added: an "Audio log (crackling sound)" switch** (launcher, Advanced / Debug, `BB_AUDIO_LOG`): logs each late sound buffer (and whether the game's sound thread or the port's timer was late), each dropout, repeated buffers and a sound timing summary once a minute, at most one line a second. Off by default; turn it on for a crackling report.

## 1. Crash Analysis & Resolutions (Wolf Enemy Combat & Stability)

### A. Dynamic VRAM GC Thresholds (`gpu/shadps4/video_core/texture_cache/texture_cache.cpp`)
- **Root Cause**: On 4 GB GPUs like the GTX 1650, texture cache garbage collection was set to trigger critical eviction only at 97% of VRAM. When the player entered combat with the first wolf enemy, dynamic particle effects, blood decals, and new texture allocations pushed VRAM beyond 100%, triggering `vk::Result=ErrorOutOfDeviceMemory STOP: GPU library assertion failed`.
- **Change**: Configured safe, progressive dynamic GC thresholds (70% trigger, 80% pressure, 88% critical eviction), fully configurable via environment variables (`BB_GC_TRIGGER`, `BB_GC_PRESSURE`, `BB_GC_CRITICAL`). This ensures stale images are evicted smoothly before GPU memory is exhausted.
- **Presenter Resilience** (`gpu/shadps4/video_core/renderer_vulkan/vk_presenter.cpp`): Added fallback allocation without `WITHIN_BUDGET` flag and host memory fallback in `RecreateFrame` during transient memory pressure.

### B. Remnant Slice Remapping in Win32 Memory Views (`src/win32_memory.c`, `src/runtime_memory.c`)
- **Root Cause**: Windows memory virtualization using `VirtualAlloc2` placeholders and `MapViewOfFile3` was dropping entire 64 KiB section views when guest code partially unmapped an allocation, failing to preserve and remap remnant prefix and suffix slices.
- **Change**: Implemented view slice tracking and automatic remnant remapping with `restore_remnant()`. Added a 64-entry lock-free ring buffer tracking recent memory operations (`g_mem_op_ring`), dumped during crash diagnosis.

### C. Removal of Silent Null Skips in VEH (`src/probe.c`)
- **Root Cause**: The Vectored Exception Handler had silent instruction skips (`8b 04 07`, `8b 3c 19`, `c5 fa 5c`) that forced destination registers to zero. This masked upstream null pointer bugs and cascaded into all general-purpose registers (RAX, RBX, RCX, RDX, RDI, RBP) becoming 0, culminating in the crash at guest offset `0x28ce9b5`.
- **Change**: Removed silent skips. If an unhandled memory fault occurs, a full crash dump (`out/crash_<timestamp>.log`) is generated with registers, stack frames, RBP chains, and faulting thread import history before clean termination.

### D. Pipeline Cache SRT Walker Fault Handler (`gpu/shadps4/shader_recompiler/ir/passes/flatten_extended_userdata_pass.cpp`)
- **Root Cause**: Restored pipeline cache entries invoked SRT walkers without ensuring the signal handler was registered, leading to access violation crashes.
- **Change**: Added `EnsureSrtFaultHandler()` on walker registration and wrapped concurrent instruction patching in a mutex (`patch_mutex`) to support parallel draw-preparation workers.

### E. AvPlayer Thread Safety (`gpu/shim/core/libraries/kernel/threads.h`)
- **Root Cause**: AvPlayer demuxer and decoder threads attempted to join themselves or collided with owner destructors during playback teardown.
- **Change**: Implemented thread-safe `Take()`/`Finish()` pattern under `std::mutex` ensuring clean detachment and non-reentrant joining.

### F. High-Entropy ASLR & Address Space Collision (Error 487) (`gpu/CMakeLists.txt`, `src/win32_memory.c`, `src/probe.c`, `src/runtime_memory.c`)
- **Root Cause**: Windows 64-bit High-Entropy ASLR and DynamicBase were enabled by default on `bbport.exe`. Windows randomized the main thread stack and CRT heap into `[0x800000000, 0xfc00000000)` (~1 TB guest address space). When `VirtualAlloc2` attempted to reserve the full 1 TB placeholder, it collided with the preexisting thread stack and returned `Windows error 487 (ERROR_INVALID_ADDRESS)`. As a result, `space()` failed, `runtime_low_map` could not allocate, image loading fell back to host allocations exceeding 40 bits (`1<<40`), and guest thread stack allocations failed with `"Cannot allocate guest thread"`.
- **Change**:
  1. Linked `bbport.exe` with `-Wl,--disable-dynamicbase` and `-Wl,--disable-high-entropy-va` in `gpu/CMakeLists.txt`, ensuring bottom-up memory layout below 32 GB.
  2. Called `runtime_memory_reserve_space()` at the very start of `main()` in `src/probe.c` before Vulkan, driver DLLs, or background worker threads are initialized.
  3. Added a chunked placeholder fallback in `win_mem_space()`: if a single 1 TB placeholder fails due to any preexisting system or hook allocation, it queries memory and reserves all free regions in `[start, end)` as placeholders, setting `space_start` and `space_end` successfully.

## 2. Audio Subsystem Refactoring (Distortion & Stuttering Fixes)

### A. Eliminated High-Priority Spinloop (`src/runtime_audio.c`)
- **Root Cause**: `output_port` ran a tight busy-wait loop (`__builtin_ia32_pause()`) while the thread was set to `THREAD_PRIORITY_TIME_CRITICAL`. On a 4C/4T CPU, this locked one core at 100% and preempted FMOD mixer and audio worker threads, causing severe underruns, robotic voice, and distorted sound.
- **Change**: Replaced spinning `sleep_until` with `host_sleep_until_ns` waitable timers and demoted audio thread priority to `THREAD_PRIORITY_HIGHEST`.

### B. Queue Steering & Cadence Alignment (`src/runtime_audio.c`)
- **Change**: Restored queue steering against the hardware WASAPI clock. The minimum queue level over a 32-buffer window is tracked and steered towards a 2-period target buffer via `adjust_ns` (±3% cadence corrections), absorbing clock drift between CPU QPC and audio DAC clocks.
- **Prefill Headroom**: Added `BB_AUDIO_PREFILL` environment variable support (default 2 periods) for configurable buffer headroom.

### C. Soft-Knee Saturation Limiter (`src/runtime_audio.c`)
- **Root Cause**: 8-channel audio was hard-clipped at `[-1.0, 1.0]`, causing square-wave clipping and harsh buzzing during multi-channel audio peaks.
- **Change**: Replaced hard clipping with a smooth soft-knee saturation curve (`soft_clip`), preventing square-wave distortion while preserving audio dynamic range.

### D. Scratch Buffer in AJM Decoding (`src/runtime_ajm.c`)
- **Change**: Replaced per-job heap `malloc`/`free` calls during multi-chunk input decoding with a 4096-byte stack scratch buffer, reducing heap allocation overhead during ATRAC9 audio playback.

## 5. Save Data Mount & Performance Regression Fixes

### A. Title Screen "Failed to create save data" Fix (`src/runtime_savedata.c`)
- **Root Cause**: `exports[]` in `src/runtime_savedata.c` only matched old `#p#J` hashes. Bloodborne's EBOOT imports `#O#P` NIDs (e.g., `ZkZhskCPXFw#O#P` for `sceSaveDataInitialize`, `32HQAQdwM2o#O#P` for `sceSaveDataMount`). Because of this mismatch, all save data calls resolved to NULL and executed unbound stubs, leaving `/savedata0` unmounted and halting the game at the title screen with *"Failed to create save data. Press OK to try again."*
- **Change**: Added all `#O#P` NIDs and plaintext symbol names to `exports[]`. Enhanced `make_dirs()` to handle both Windows backslashes (`\`) and POSIX forward slashes (`/`).

### B. Framerate Drop (10 FPS Regression) & CPU Spun Loops Fix (`src/runtime_kernel.c`, `src/probe.c`)
- **Root Cause**:
  1. `exports[]` in `src/runtime_kernel.c` lacked Bloodborne's actual NIDs for `sceKernelClockGettime` (`QBi7HCK03hw#p#J`), `sceKernelUsleep` (`1jfXLRVzisc#p#J`), and `sceKernelReadTsc` (`-2IRUCO--PM#p#J`). `usleep` returned 0 without sleeping, spinning CPU cores at 100%.
  2. In `src/probe.c`, unbound import execution called `printf` and `fflush(stdout)` unconditionally on every single call without checking `g_bb_trace`. With unresolved/unbound imports being invoked in fast loops, this caused over 400,000 unbuffered console I/O writes and flushes, bottlenecking the process and dropping framerate from 30 FPS down to ~8-10 FPS.
- **Change**: Bound correct Bloodborne timer/sleep NIDs in `src/runtime_kernel.c`, and strictly gated unbound import logging in `src/probe.c` behind `if (g_bb_trace && count <= 3)` with low call caps.

## 6. Low-VRAM (4 GB) & Low-Core (4C/4T) Hardware Adaptation

### A. VRAM Budget Scaling & Thrashing Elimination (`gpu/shadps4/video_core/renderer_vulkan/vk_instance.cpp`, `vk_instance.h`, `texture_cache.cpp`)
- **Root Cause**:
  1. On discrete GPUs, `CollectPhysicalMemoryInfo` subtracted a 1 GB system memory reserve from the dedicated VRAM budget regardless of total VRAM size. On a 4 GB card (GTX 1650), this reduced the usable budget from ~3800 MiB to ~2825 MiB.
  2. In `TextureCache::GarbageCollectImages`, the default trigger threshold was hardcoded to 70% of budget (`2825 * 0.70 = 1977 MiB`), pressure at 80% (2260 MiB), and critical eviction at 88% (2486 MiB).
  3. Bloodborne's base working set in Iosefka's Clinic is ~2500–2600 MiB. On 8 GB GPUs (like the RX 6600), the 70% threshold is ~4900 MiB, so GC never triggers (0 evictions). On 4 GB GPUs, 2500 MiB immediately exceeded the 88% critical mark, forcing the texture cache into a permanent critical eviction panic.
  4. Every 5 seconds, 1389 images were evicted and downloaded back across PCIe using `scheduler.Finish()`, stalling the CPU/GPU pipelines and tanking frame rate from 30 FPS down to 10 FPS.
  5. During combat with the Scourge Beast, particle stream buffers and blood decal textures were evicted while being accessed by guest draw routines, leading to page-watcher invalidation and guest register corruption (`address 0` fault at `0x28ce9b5`).
- **Change**:
  1. Exposed `GetDeviceLocalMemory()` in `Instance` to query physical dedicated VRAM size.
  2. In `CollectPhysicalMemoryInfo()`, restricted the 1 GB system reserve subtraction to GPUs with > 4 GB VRAM, preserving the full physical VRAM pool for <= 4 GB cards.
  3. Dynamically scale GC budget on <= 4 GB GPUs up to 88% of physical VRAM (~3600 MiB on 4 GB cards), with dynamic low-VRAM thresholds: trigger at 88% (3168 MiB), pressure at 93% (3348 MiB), and critical at 97% (3492 MiB). This allows Bloodborne's 2500–2600 MiB working set to remain resident without thrashing.
  4. Protected recently touched images from aggressive eviction by raising `ticks_to_destroy` to 900 ticks (30 seconds) in aggressive mode and 1800 ticks (60 seconds) in pressured mode, preventing active combat encounter assets from being destroyed.
  5. Throttled synchronous downloads (`gc_downloads`) to at most 1 per frame to eliminate multiple `scheduler.Finish()` pipeline flushes.

### B. 4C/4T CPU Thread Scheduling & Starvation Resolution (`src/runtime_thread.c`, `src/host_sync.h`)
- **Root Cause**:
  1. `ps4_prio_to_win32` assigned `THREAD_PRIORITY_HIGHEST` to any thread with priority `<= 260`. Bloodborne spawns over 20 background worker threads (5 `GXWorker`, `HavokWorkerThread`, 6 `CSChrThread`, 6 `CSClothThread`, 6 `SpClothVertexUpdate`) with priority `256`.
  2. On a 12-thread CPU (Ryzen 5600), hardware threads handle these workers easily. On a 4-core / 4-thread CPU (i5-4590), 20 workers running at `THREAD_PRIORITY_HIGHEST` completely preempted and starved the main game loop thread and Vulkan presenter (running at default `THREAD_PRIORITY_NORMAL`).
  3. `host_yield()` called Win32 `SwitchToThread()`, which yields only to threads on the same physical core; when no other thread is scheduled on that core, it returns immediately without yielding, causing high-CPU spin contention.
- **Change**:
  1. Added dynamic CPU core count detection (`get_host_cores()`). On systems with 4 cores or fewer, worker pool threads (`prio >= 250`) are assigned `THREAD_PRIORITY_NORMAL` (or `BELOW_NORMAL`), while audio threads maintain elevated priority.
  2. In `runtime_thread_attach_main()`, elevated the main game loop thread to `THREAD_PRIORITY_ABOVE_NORMAL`, preventing background workers from starving the main loop on low-core CPUs.
  3. In `host_sync.h`, updated `host_yield()` to fall back to `Sleep(0)` when `SwitchToThread()` fails, giving up the time slice to any ready thread across all cores.

## 7. Combat / Hit Blood Particle Generator Stack Unwind (`src/probe.c`)

### A. Clean Frame Restoration for Impact Particle Generation (`0x28ce7b0 .. 0x28ceeb8`)
- **Root Cause**:
  1. Function `0x28ce7b0` is the blood splatter/impact vertex stream generator invoked when any enemy lands a hit on the character.
  2. The function relies on the System V AMD64 128-byte Red Zone (`[rsp - 0x60]`, `[rsp - 0x38]`, etc.). On Windows, memory pressure / write-tracking faults invoke kernel exception dispatch (`KiUserExceptionDispatch`), which allocates 1.3 KB below `RSP`, clobbering these Red Zone local pointers.
  3. A prior recovery attempted to branch mid-function to `0x28cea00`. Jumping into the middle of the loop skipped stream initialization and cascaded into reading `[rcx + rdx] = [0 + 4] = 0x4` at `0x28cea45`.
- **Change**:
  1. Updated `win_veh_handler` in `src/probe.c`: if any fault occurs within the entire function body (`0x28ce7ba .. 0x28ceeb8`), it safely reads the preserved callee-saved registers from the stack frame (`RBX`, `R12`, `R13`, `R14`, `R15`, `RBP`), pops the return address, restores `RSP = RSP + 0x68`, and returns directly to the caller (`0x2ab485c` / `0x2ac458e`).
  2. The caller's code immediately completes the hit reaction and frame without crashing, allowing gameplay to proceed smoothly on all enemy hits.

## 9. 0.1% and 1.0% Frametime Stutter Elimination & Performance Enhancements

### A. Critical Path Thread Priority Scheduling (`src/runtime_thread.c`)
- **Root Cause**: On 4-core / 4-thread CPUs, `ps4_prio_to_win32()` assigned `THREAD_PRIORITY_NORMAL` to all threads with `prio <= 260` (the 12 background worker threads: `EzWorkPool:0..3`, `GXWorker:0..5`, `ClothWorker`, `HavokWorker`), but downgraded threads with `prio <= 700` to `THREAD_PRIORITY_BELOW_NORMAL`. Bloodborne's primary rendering thread (`GXRenderThread`) and main game loop (`MOMainThread`) are created with PS4 default `prio = 700`. As a result, the critical GPU submission thread ran at lower priority than 12 background workers. During enemy encounters and physics simulations, the workers preempted `GXRenderThread`, causing rendering stalls (`== Stall during rendering at flush 1046`), pipeline bubbles, and severe 1% / 0.1% low frametime drops.
- **Change**:
  1. Explicitly elevated critical path rendering and main threads (`GXRenderThread`, `RenderThread`, `Present`, `Flip`, `Display`, `MOMainThread`, `MainThread`) to `THREAD_PRIORITY_ABOVE_NORMAL`.
  2. Demoted background worker pools (`Worker`, `Pool`, `Work`, `Cloth`, `Havok`, `Job`) to `THREAD_PRIORITY_BELOW_NORMAL` on systems with $\le$ 4 cores, ensuring the GPU command submission pipeline is never starved or preempted.
  3. Updated `thread_rename()` to dynamically update Win32 thread priority if a thread was created anonymously and renamed after startup.

### B. Texture Cache Anti-Thrashing & Calibrated VRAM Thresholds (`gpu/shadps4/video_core/texture_cache/texture_cache.cpp`)
- **Root Cause**: On 4 GB discrete GPUs (e.g. GTX 1650), Bloodborne's active scene memory hovers around 3.1–3.2 GB. Under the previous calculation, `pressure_gc_memory` was dynamically clamped to 3,163 MiB. Because memory usage hovered around 3,216 MiB, the collector remained permanently in `pressured = true`. In that state, `ticks_to_destroy` was set to 80 ticks (~0.2s / 3 frames), causing any texture temporarily out of camera view to be evicted. In 5 seconds, over 3,600 images were evicted and downloaded synchronously via `DownloadImageMemory(image_id, true)` (calling `scheduler.Finish()` multiple times per frame). On subsequent frames, these same textures had to be re-allocated, staged, and re-uploaded over PCIe, creating recurring 150–300 ms pipeline freezes.
- **Change**:
  1. Recalibrated thresholds on low-VRAM GPUs: `trigger_gc_memory` (~70% / 2,860 MiB) allows the 20-second idle collector (`BB_GC_IDLE_SECONDS=20`) to reclaim stale area textures smoothly; `pressure_gc_memory` (~90% / 3,680 MiB) prevents false emergency triggers during standard rendering; `critical_gc_memory` (~95% / 3,890 MiB) acts as a safety backstop.
  2. Replaced tick-based age under pressure with real-time second boundaries: pressured eviction only targets textures idle for $\ge$ 3–5 seconds (`pressure_tick`), while critical eviction targets textures idle for $\ge$ 1 second (`critical_tick`). Active view frustum textures are never evicted.
  3. Rate-limited synchronous GPU downloads (`scheduler.Finish()`) to at most 1 per submission pass and limited deletions per pass (4 normal, 6 pressure, 16 critical), eliminating pipeline stall cascades.
