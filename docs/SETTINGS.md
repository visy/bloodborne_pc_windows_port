# Settings, launcher and controls

Back to the [README](../README.md).

## In-game settings

- **Settings inside the game's own menu**: every setting of the port is a page of the game's *System* menu (after *Environment*, i.e. Screen/Sound, on the title screen and in the pause menu), built and drawn by the game itself, in the game's language (English or Russian): *Display* (output resolution, preset, sharpness, motion blur resolution, model detail, upscaler), *Effects* (the graphics patches), *Upscaler* (jitter, character motion vectors, reactive mask, FSR 4 options, live resolution), *Advanced* (reactive mask tuning, async shaders, pipeline libraries, debug view), *Frame generation* (DLSS / FSR frame generation, NVIDIA Reflex; after a restart, see [FG_REFLEX.md](FG_REFLEX.md)), *HDR* (HDR output, peak brightness, paper white, highlights, saturation, calibration pattern; see [HDR output](#hdr-output)), *Performance HUD* (on/off, opacity, size, shader indicator, the port's language, position), *HUD details* (graph, VRAM, GPU, CPU, RAM, FPS counter), *Mouse & keyboard* (on/off, sensitivity, invert), *Mouse tuning* (smoothing, dead zone, the wheel as quick items), *Game patches* (the game's own AA, skip intros, free camera, debug menu, *Restart the game*, *Quit the game*), *Party*, *Party mode*, *Party members* and *Party network* (see [docs/PARTY.md](docs/PARTY.md)), the host's rules (*Party rules: World*, *Party rules: PvP & lamps*, *Party rules: Deaths*, *Party rules: Rot*, *Party rules: Enemies*; a guest sees the host's values read-only) and each player's own party settings (*My party: Deaths*, *My party: World*, *My party: Story*, *My party: Rewards*, *My party: Display*, *My party: Look & notices*): every party gameplay option is a row there and a checkbox in the launcher's *Party Rules* or *My Party* tab, kept in `bbport.ini`. Changes apply at once and are saved to `bbport.ini`; the bottom line explains each row (slider positions included). Buttons are rows switched with left/right; *Leave*, *Kick*, *Restart* and *Quit* need a second press. The game's *Default* button restores the port's defaults. `BB_GAME_MENU=0` leaves the game's menu untouched.
- **In-Game Overlay**: Press `Insert` (or `L3 + R3` on your controller, when enabled in the launcher) to open the overlay menu, styled like the game's dialogs: the same settings with longer descriptions, with gamepad, keyboard and mouse. It is a fallback (and a debugging aid); normal settings are all in the game's own menu.
- **Mouse & keyboard mode** (opt-in: launcher, the menu, or `mk_enabled=1`; `BB_MOUSE_KEYBOARD=1` for scripts): the mouse turns the camera and attacks, the wheel steps the quick items and tools (d-pad up / down, one press per notch; `mk_wheel=0` turns it off). The Insert menu's *Controls* tab rebinds keys (see [Controls](#controls)); `mkkey.<input>=` lines in `bbport.ini` rebind this layout; mouse buttons and the wheel are names there too (`Mouse Left`, `Mouse Right`, `Mouse Middle`, `Mouse X1`, `Mouse X2`, `Wheel Up`, `Wheel Down`), and a mouse button bound this way leaves the built-in mouse scheme (e.g. `mkkey.square=R, Mouse X1`). From upstream 0.51 (#51, #52); see [UPSTREAM_0.51_INTEGRATION.md](UPSTREAM_0.51_INTEGRATION.md).

**Motion blur resolution** (System > *Display* > *Motion blur resolution*, the overlay's Display tab, the launcher's *Motion blur resolution*, `bbport.ini` `volumetric_res=0|1|2`, `BB_VOLUMETRIC_RES`; 0.6.2 called it *Volumetric effects*; idea from a playtester): the one reduced-resolution chain in Bloodborne's frame is its motion blur: a half-size depth/stencil made from the scene depth, a velocity buffer drawn with it by the moving characters and objects, two dilation passes and a half-size velocity/depth buffer, which two full-size passes sample to blur the scene. Smoke, mist and fog sprites are not in it: the game draws them blended into the full-size scene, so they are already at the scene's resolution (frame captures in Hunter's Dream, Central Yharnam, Cathedral Ward, Forbidden Woods, Hemwick and Nightmare Frontier, 0.6.3). *Game default* (0, the default) keeps the half size; *2x pixels* (1) renders the chain at 1.4x the size per axis, *Full resolution* (2) at the scene's size. It changes live and only shows while things move (finer blur edges around moving characters); with the *Motion blur* effect off there is nothing to scale. The game keeps its own allocations: the port draws the chain into larger proxies with the viewport, scissor and pixel coordinates converted, and the blur passes sample them; anything reading these buffers otherwise (compute, integer loads, copies) gets them scaled back to the game's size. Not scaled: light shafts / glare and the bloom pyramid (the post-processing's own fixed-size buffers), SSAO, the quarter-size depth, and a half-size pass the Forbidden Woods draw before the scene (a compute pass clears its memory as a buffer). The launcher's *Motion blur resolution* reads and writes the same `volumetric_res`; `BB_VOLUMETRIC_RES` (scripts, tests) wins over it.

Output resolution (in-game Display page and System menu) also offers ultrawide sizes, 21:9 (2560x1080, 3440x1440, 3840x1600, 5120x2160) and 32:9 (3840x1080, 5120x1440); they apply after a restart (startup patch, never live) and need an upscaler other than TAA.

### HDR output

Optional, **off by default**: an HDR10 picture (SMPTE ST 2084 PQ, Rec.2020 primaries) for an HDR display. Turn it on in the launcher (*Graphics > HDR output (HDR10)*), in the game's *System > HDR* page or the Insert menu (`hdr=1` in `bbport.ini`, `BB_HDR=1`); it applies at the next start and needs **HDR on in Windows** (*Settings > Display > Use HDR*) for the game's display. Bloodborne itself renders an SDR picture; the port keeps it as it is and expands it at the very end:

- the game's colours are decoded as the TV gamma 2.2 they were graded for, then only the highlights are expanded: everything below about 73 % of the SDR signal (linear luminance 0.5) stays where **paper white** puts it, brighter parts rise smoothly towards the **peak**, on luminance only (a lamp's orange stays orange, no hue shift, no channel clipping), converted to Rec.2020 and PQ encoded; colours above the peak are scaled down as a whole;
- the port's own overlay (menus, party HUD and name tags, performance HUD, notices and captions) is drawn at paper white, never blown out, and blends as it does in SDR;
- while one of the game's full-screen menus is up (pause / inventory, equipment, status, System, the lamp's travel list) the highlights fade out over a quarter second, so the game's white menu text sits at paper white like any HDR game's UI, and fade back in when it closes (`BB_HDR_MENU_SDR=0` keeps them; the calibration pattern always shows the curve as set);
- works with every upscaler (TAA, FSR 3.1, FSR 4, DLSS), with DLSS / FSR frame generation and with the ultrawide outputs: the HDR pass runs after them on the final picture;
- when the display or driver offers no HDR10 swapchain, or HDR is off in Windows, the game presents SDR as before (with the `BB_HDR_GAMMA` correction on an HDR desktop) and *System > HDR* says why (log: `HDR: output asked for but not used: ...`).

| Setting (System > HDR) | `bbport.ini` | Environment | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| HDR output (after a restart) | `hdr=0/1` | `BB_HDR` | 0 |
| Peak brightness (cd/m2; Auto = what the display reports to Windows, else 1000) | `hdr_peak_nits=auto/400..10000` | `BB_HDR_PEAK` | auto |
| Paper white (mid-tones and the port's menus) | `hdr_paper_white=80..500` | `BB_HDR_PAPER_WHITE` | 200 |
| Highlights (0 = the SDR look at paper white, 1 = the game's white reaches the peak, up to 1.5) | `hdr_strength` | `BB_HDR_STRENGTH` | 1.0 |
| Saturation (1 = the game's colours; above 1 uses the wider Rec.2020 gamut) | `hdr_saturation=0.5..1.5` | `BB_HDR_SATURATION` | 1.0 |
| Calibration pattern (not saved) | - | `BB_HDR_PATTERN=1` | off |

The **calibration pattern** fills a band at the top of the screen (the System menu stays usable below it): a 10000-nit field with a square at the peak setting (raise *Peak brightness* until the square just disappears into the field: that is your display's real peak), a paper white patch, a ramp from black to the peak, and the game's grey levels 0, 0.1 ... 1.0 through the current curve. Test aids: `BB_HDR_FORCE=1` creates the HDR10 swapchain even with HDR off in Windows (Windows then maps it down), `BB_HDR_FORMAT=scrgb` prefers scRGB (`R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT`, extended linear sRGB) over HDR10, `BB_HDR_DUMP_TRIGGER=<file>` saves the SDR frame and the encoded HDR image once the file appears (checked by `tools/windows/hdr_check.py`), `BB_HDR_TEST_FILE=<file>` changes the curve from a file (`pattern`, `peak=N`, `paper=N`, `strength=F`, `saturation=F`). The log lists the swapchain formats the display offers (`Swapchain: formats offered:`) and the display's reported peak (`Display: HDR on in Windows ..., peak N nits reported`). shadPS4's passthrough for PS4 games that output HDR themselves is `BB_GAME_HDR=1` (Bloodborne does not).

## Launcher

Double-click `launch_gui.bat` or run:
```cmd
launch_gui.bat
```
- Select your `eboot.bin` file or game folder.
- **Display**: target frame rate (60 FPS recommended), render resolution, window mode (`BB_WINDOW_MODE`: *Borderless* (default), *Windowed* or exclusive *Fullscreen*, which can crash on laptops with hybrid graphics), monitor (`BB_DISPLAY`), frames ahead (1 = least input lag, 2 = default, 3 = smoothest).
- **Graphics**: *Enable upscaler* with its choice (FSR 3.1 / FSR 4 / FSR 4.1.1 / TAA / DLSS, the game menu's list; unchecked = `upscaler=off`; the launcher cannot check the GPU, so it lists all of them and the game falls back to FSR 3.1 when FSR 4 / 4.1.1 or DLSS is not available), object motion vectors, anisotropic filtering, frame generation (Off / DLSS FG 2x-4x / FSR FG 2x) and NVIDIA Reflex (Off / On / On + Boost; both game settings in `bbport.ini`, off by default, see [FG_REFLEX.md](FG_REFLEX.md)), HDR output with its peak brightness and paper white ([HDR output](#hdr-output); off by default), VRAM pre-upload (*Full* needs a 12 GB+ GPU).
- **Performance & Shaders**: *Performance Patch*, draw preparation workers, shader precompile, async shaders, GPL, the compile indicator, and **Export / Import shader cache**.
- **Game**: skip intro, skip the online/offline choice, settings in the game's System menu, mods, and *All XML Patches...*.
- **Input & Audio**: controller (`BB_GAMEPAD`), overlay on `L3 + R3`, mute in background.
- **Advanced / Debug**: diagnostics, watchdog, party logging, support bundle, and the [shader cache builder](#shader-cache-builder-world-warper).
- **Party**: host or join a co-op party, the party code, password and connection settings, *Party help* ([guide](docs/PARTY.md)).
- **Party Rules**: what the host decides for the whole party (shared world, friendly fire and lamps, the host's deaths, rot); guests follow the host's. In the game: *System > Party rules: ...*.
- **My Party**: each player's own party settings (party HUD, name tags, pings, glow, notices, story sync, what you get as a guest, party saves). In the game: *System > My party: ...*.
- Hover any option for details, then click **Launch Bloodborne**. `run.bat` started on its own uses the same saved settings.

**Game settings and launch options.** A setting that is also in the game's menus (System pages, Insert overlay) lives only in `bbport.ini`: the launcher reads it when it opens, writes it when you change it, and reads it again when the game exits (and when its window gets the focus after the file changed), so both always show the same value; it sets no `BB_*` variable for it at launch (the variables still work for scripts and tests and then win over the ini). These are: the upscaler (`upscaler`), object motion vectors (`object_motion`), motion blur resolution (`volumetric_res`), the performance HUD (`show_hud`), mouse & keyboard mode (`mk_enabled`), button prompts (`prompt_style`), async shaders (`async_pipelines`), pipeline libraries (`gpl`), the shader indicator (`compile_indicator`), frame generation, Reflex, HDR, the party's HUD / rules switches (0.7.1 added `party_kill_record`, `party_gather`, `party_npc_mirror` on *Shared world* (0.7.3 `party_travel_vote` there, off by default) and `party_key_view`, `party_key_items_summoned` on *Party rewards*, all on by default), and the patches the game's *Effects* page switches (*Skip Intro* = `skip_intro`, *Disable Motion Blur* = `effect_motion_blur=0`, the other effect patches and the model LOD patches of *All XML Patches...*). Everything else (frame rate and FPS patch, frame hooks, render resolution, window mode, monitor, controller, VRAM pre-upload, frames ahead, anisotropic filtering, draw prep workers, shader precompile, the overlay switches, mute in background, mods, *Performance Patch* and other XML patches, diagnostics, the Party tab's connection) is a launch option kept in `launcher_settings.json`. The first start after the update moves the old launcher values into `bbport.ini` once: a key the ini lacks, or a launcher value that used to override the ini at every launch (e.g. an unchecked upscaler, a checked *Skip Intro*); after that the ini wins.

### Shader cache builder (world warper)

*Advanced / Debug > Shader cache builder > Build shader cache (world warper)...* fills the shader cache by playing the game for you, so later play stutters less in places you have not been yet:

- **What it does**: the game starts in a test mode (`BB_WORLD_WARPER=tour`), continues your save on the title screen and then travels through every ReturnPointParam row with a spot - the Hunter's Dream, all 44 lamps and 23 boss-arena / event respawn points (68 places in all, the Old Hunters DLC included; the Chalice Dungeons only with the `chalice` option and a save that has a dungeon) - with the game's own lamp warp and one normal loading screen each. On arriving in each place it puts on the next armor set and a right-hand and left-hand weapon (as the equipment menu does; every set and weapon model comes round once in a tour), turns the camera all the way round, walks a loop, uses the weapon in both forms and the firearm (and, once, the quick items), plays the place's cutscenes once, fights every kind of enemy within 45 m that the run has not met yet and kills it, and in the first place shows every item effect with a look of its own (Blood Vial, papers, pellets, Old Hunter Bone...). Then it visits each Yharnam map again at the other times of day (day, evening, night, Blood Moon) and puts the save copy's own time back. Your HP is kept full meanwhile.
- **Your saves are never changed**: it plays on a **copy** of your save folder (`user\world_warper\savedata`, made when you start; the warper moves the copy's character, uses its items and changes its last lamp). The shader cache it fills is your own (`user\cache`).
- **Progress**: a panel at the top of the game window (place n / 68, the area, what it does, the pipelines new to the cache here and in all) and a line per place in the launcher log (`World warper: [12/68] Old Yharnam, row 2302950 (m23_00_00_00): +N new pipelines (in the world: enemy ..., look ...)`).
- **Stop and resume**: *Stop* ends it; pressing the button again asks whether to continue where it stopped (`user\world_warper\world_warper_state.txt`) or start over. If the game crashes the launcher starts it again and it continues; a place it crashed in twice is skipped.
- **The result**: at the end the game closes and the launcher exports the shader cache as one `.zip` (the *Export shader cache* format) where you chose when starting. It is made from the game's shaders: use it yourself or share it only with people who own the game. The project never publishes such caches.
- It takes two to three hours (a full run on the test PC: see below). Leave the window alone meanwhile; mouse, keyboard and pad input would mix with the warper's own.

Full run (test PC, 30 FPS test settings, an empty cache, 2026-10-15): all 75 places (with `chalice`) and 33 time-of-day visits, 54 enemy kinds, 36 cutscenes, 17 item effects; the cache ended with 801 pipelines and 856 shaders. New pipelines per place, added up: 51 after 5 places, 157 after 20, 269 after 40, 312 after 50, 330 after 75, and none in the time-of-day visits (the light changes, the shaders do not): the last third of the tour and the time-of-day pass found almost nothing new.

Earlier MVP measurement (test PC, 30 FPS test settings, an empty cache): the tour made 425 pipelines new to the cache in 56 places (most from enemies, 154, and the camera sweeps, 114; the rest on the loading screens and while walking and attacking). A replay of the same walk through two places before and after the tour in one session: 61 -> 1 new pipelines, 53 -> 1 draws skipped for a pipeline still compiling, 342 -> 2 ms of compiling on the GPU thread. Long frames right after a loading screen (texture and map streaming) are not shader stutter and stay; the log counts both (`hitches over 50 ms`, and those with a pipeline made in the frame).

Test aids: `BB_WORLD_WARPER=tour|replay|measure` with `,lamps=A+B`, `,replay=A+B`, `,max=N`, `,noact`, `,noenemy`, `,nokill`, `,enemies=N`, `,chalice`, `,notod`, `,nogear`, `,noremo`, `,bosses` (bosses too: off by default; work in progress), `,noitems`, `,dump`; work in progress, not verified live: `,gestures`, `,tools`, `,remopass`, `,remoonly`, `,remos=A+B`, `,remoskip=S`; `BB_WORLD_WARPER_STATE=<file>` (see [party/DEBUG_SWITCHES.md](party/DEBUG_SWITCHES.md)). `measure` runs the replay, the tour, and the replay again in one session and logs `stutter check: replay before / after the tour`.

### Shader caches: what they are and what can be shared

The port keeps its shader cache in `user\cache\CUSA03173\` (`BB_USER_DIR` / `BB_GPU_USER_DIR` move it):

| Files | What | Works on |
|---|---|---|
| `*.key` | the pipelines the game asked for (shaders + render state) | every GPU |
| `*.src` | the game's own shader code, as the port saw it | every GPU |
| `*.meta`, `*.spv`, `profile.bin` | the shaders translated for this GPU's features (`profile.bin`) | the same GPU profile only; elsewhere the game translates them again from the `.src` files before the intro |
| `vk_pipe_cache_VVVV_DDDD_XXXXXXXX.vkp` | the driver's own compiled pipelines (VkPipelineCache), named by vendor, device and driver version | the same GPU, device and driver only (its header is checked; another one is ignored) |

The GPU driver keeps a cache of its own as well (NVIDIA: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\NVIDIA\DXCache` / `GLCache`); that one is per PC and cannot be shared.

**Sharing**: *Export shader cache* writes `.key`, `.src`, `.meta`, `.spv` and `profile.bin`. `python tools/cache_bundle.py <cache root> out.zip` makes a bundle for other PCs: the GPU-independent part at the top, this GPU's part (with the `.vkp`) in `gpu\<vendor>_<vendor id>-<device id>_driver_<version>\`, and a README. To use a shared `.zip`: drop it into `user\cache_import\` (the launcher merges it at the next launch and renames it `.zip.imported`) or use *Import shader cache...*. Nothing already there is overwritten; the GPU part is taken only when its `profile.bin` matches yours (or you have no cache yet: the game then checks it itself). Shader caches are made from the game's own shaders: share them only privately; the project never commits or publishes them.

## Controls

- **Gamepad**: Controllers are supported out of the box through SDL3. With several connected, `BB_GAMEPAD=<GUID or part of the name>` picks one. The keyboard works too, also next to a connected gamepad. The character name is typed on the keyboard in a box over the game.
- **Touchpad**: its left half (`Tab` / `G`, Back/Select) opens the gestures, the right half (`Backspace`) the key items. Pads without a touch surface (Xbox and other XInput pads): `Back` + d-pad left / right clicks the left / right half for as long as the d-pad is held, and `Back` alone clicks the left half when it is released (so `Back` + right never opens the gestures first). While `Back` is held, d-pad left / right do not switch items. This applies while `Back` is bound to nothing but the touchpad (the default); a `pad.<input>=` line that moves `Back` elsewhere keeps the plain rule, and pads with a real touchpad are unchanged.
- **Walk key** (after Mrsuss60/bloodborne_pc_windows_port 5e029181 by HatsyRei): hold `Left Alt` while moving with the keyboard to walk instead of run, in both keyboard layouts. The keyboard's move stick then goes half way (64 of 127) instead of all the way: the game ignores the stick up to about 40, walks from 48 to at least 115 (about 1.5 m/s) and runs only near full deflection (3.2-3.6 m/s; measured 2026-10-17). It is rebound like the other keys (*Walk (hold while moving)* in the Insert menu's *Controls* tab, `key.walk=` / `mkkey.walk=`); it is not a PS4 button and has no `pad.` line (push the gamepad's stick part way to walk). `Alt + Enter` still toggles fullscreen.
- **In-Game Settings**: *System* in the game's own menu (*Display*, *Effects*, *Upscaler*, *Advanced*, *HDR*, *Performance HUD*, *HUD details*, *Mouse & keyboard*, *Mouse tuning*, *Game patches*, *Party*, *Party members*), or press `Insert` on keyboard (`L3 + R3` on gamepad when enabled in the launcher) to toggle the overlay menu (its last row, *Quit the game*, closes the game after a second press).
- **Key bindings**: the Insert menu's *Controls* tab lists every input of the layout in use with its keys. Select a row (mouse, Enter or Cross), then press the new key, or in mouse & keyboard mode a mouse button or the wheel: it replaces that input's keys. If another input had that key, it moves over and the other input gets this one's old first key ("Q taken from R3 (lock-on); it gets X"). `Esc` (gamepad Circle) cancels, `Delete` unbinds, *Default keys* removes the layout's lines. The rows write `mkkey.<input>=` lines (mouse & keyboard mode) or `key.<input>=` lines (classic layout) to `bbport.ini`, which the game uses at once; in mouse & keyboard mode the mouse buttons' built-in roles show in the list (left R1, right L2, middle R3, X1 L1, X2 R2) and a button bound to something else leaves them (and its Shift + click). Gamepad `pad.<input>=` lines are edited in the file only. Key names in these lines are SDL's (`Right Shift`, `Return`, `Escape`, `Backspace`, letters), any case, or the short names `rshift`, `lshift`, `shift`, `enter`, `return`, `esc`, `escape`, `ctrl`, `lctrl`, `rctrl`, `alt`, `lalt`, `ralt`, `altgr`, `bksp`, `del`, `ins`, `pgup`, `pgdn`, `pgdown`; several keys are separated by commas (`key.touchpad=Tab, rshift`). An unknown name is logged (`controls: unknown key`) and skipped.
- **Party hotkeys** (in a party; they come on top of the bindings above and are not rebound by them): a **ping** is the touchpad held + R3 (pads without a touch surface: `Back` held + R3; keyboard: the touchpad key + the R3 key, e.g. `Tab` + `Q`, or `V`), **Gather the party** (the host only, as *System → Party → Gather the party*: each guest is asked, the same refusals and the 30 s wait) is the touchpad held + L3 (`Back` held + L3; keyboard: `Tab` + the L3 key, e.g. `Tab` + `Z`, or `H`). The stick click of a combo does not reach the game; `V` and `H` still do what a `key.*` / `mkkey.*` line binds them to, and on a guest `H` is only the game's key. `BB_PARTY_PINGS=0` / `BB_PARTY_GATHER=0` turn them off with their features.
- **Button prompts** (ported from deadinside28's 0.6-pre1, bloodborne_pc 72e5c8ce): the game's key guide icons (the 22 `KG_*` icons of `menu/common.tpf.dcx` and the HUD's prompt icons in `MENU_Common_00091`) are recognised by their bytes when the game uploads them (read from the game's own file at start, mods included) and drawn again by the port for the device used last: the bound key (short names on the caps: `Esc`, `Bksp`, arrows; Space, Enter and Shift as their signs), the mouse button in mouse & keyboard mode (left R1, Shift + left R2, right L2, Shift + right L1, middle R3, or a bound one), or the gamepad's button. PlayStation pads (SDL types PS3 / PS4 / PS5) keep the game's own icons; Switch Pro, Joy-Con and GameCube pads get Nintendo letters, L / R / ZL / ZR, + and -; every other pad Xbox letters (A B X Y in their colours), LB / RB / LT / RT, Menu and View. Letters come from SDL's labels for the pad type, not the position: the game's confirm button (Cross, the bottom button) shows A on Xbox and B on Nintendo, and what each button does never changes. The d-pad keeps the game's icons on every pad. Setting `prompt_style=auto|playstation|xbox|nintendo|keyboard` (launcher *Input & Audio > Button prompts*, game menu *System > Mouse & keyboard > Button prompts*, Insert *Controls*; `BB_PROMPT_STYLE` overrides at start): *Auto* follows the device and the pad's type; *PlayStation*, *Xbox* and *Nintendo* pick the gamepad's set (the keyboard still shows keys while it was used last); *Keyboard* shows keys always. A running game reads a changed `prompt_style` from `bbport.ini` within a second. `BB_BUTTON_PROMPTS=0` turns the feature off. Example: an 8BitDo pad in X-input mode (USB id 2dc8:3106) reports itself to SDL as an Xbox One pad with A at the bottom, so *Auto* shows Xbox letters even where the pad prints B; choose *Nintendo* for it. The game log line `gamepad connected: <name>, VID:PID ..., SDL type ..., button prompts ...` shows what a pad reports.
- **Mouse look** (mouse & keyboard mode): the mouse moves the right stick. The game ignores the stick up to 46.7 of 127 (a round dead zone), turns 1.13 deg/s per unit above it up to 120 (83 deg/s) and jumps to its fast turn (181 deg/s) near full deflection (measured with `BB_CAM_PROBE=1`, 2026-10-15); the port maps the mouse through the inverse of that curve, so the camera turns about 0.085 deg per mouse count at sensitivity 1 (measured 83-87 mdeg from 100 to 800 px/s; linear in the slider) and the fastest motion gets the fast turn. *Direct mouse camera* turns the camera without the stick at all.
- **Free Camera**: When enabled in the launcher or overlay, hold `Cross` and press `L3` (or hold `Space` and press `Z` on keyboard) to cycle camera modes. Uses Lance McDonald's GoldHEN patch; conflicts with *Enemy Control*.
- **Debug Menu**: If debug fonts (`DbgFont14h.ccm` and `DbgFont14h.tpf`, from [Debug Menu and XML Patch](https://www.nexusmods.com/bloodborne/mods/253)) are installed into `dvdroot_ps4/font/` (or `adhoc/font/`), open the menu using `Tab` or the left half of the controller touchpad. Startup rejects missing or empty font files instead of launching the unsafe patch.

## Environment variables

`BB_FRAME_STATS=1` (frame statistics), `BB_TRACE=1` (heartbeat and hang dumps in `out\`, cheap), `BB_TRACE_IMPORTS=1` (also logs every import call: much slower), `BB_SAVE_LOG=1` (frame and readback statistics into `logs\<time>.frames.csv` / `.readbacks.csv`; the launcher's own log is `launcher.log`), `BB_ANISO=N` (anisotropic filtering; 16 by default, 0 = the game's own), `BB_UPSCALER=taa|fsr3|fsr4|fsr411|dlss|off`, `BB_FRAMES_AHEAD=N`, `BB_PRESENT_THREAD=0`, `BB_LIVE_RES=1`, `BB_PREUPLOAD=0|1|2` (background upload of GPU memory into VRAM: 1 by default, 2 = all of it, ~3 GB more VRAM, 0 = off), `BB_READBACKS=0|1|2`, `BB_GPU_PROFILE=1`, `BB_BREADCRUMBS=0` (GPU breadcrumbs off: a device lost then names no command; its report `out\device_lost_<time>.log` is written either way), `BB_DEVICE_LOST_REPORT_TEST=S` (test: S s in, the device-lost report of a healthy GPU, plus the newest dispatch's shader files), `BB_DISPLAY`, `BB_HDR_GAMMA=0` (no gamma 2.2 correction of the SDR picture when Windows runs the display in HDR), `BB_HDR=1`, `BB_HDR_PEAK`, `BB_HDR_PAPER_WHITE`, `BB_HDR_STRENGTH`, `BB_HDR_SATURATION`, `BB_HDR_PATTERN=1`, `BB_HDR_FORCE=1`, `BB_HDR_FORMAT=scrgb`, `BB_HDR_MENU_SDR=0` (native HDR output, see [HDR output](#hdr-output)), `BB_GAMEPAD`, `BB_SKIP_NETWORK_CHOICE=0`, `BB_GAME_MENU=0`, `BB_SKIP_GAME_CHECK=1`, `BB_WINDOW_MODE=windowed|borderless|fullscreen` (fullscreen is exclusive at the desktop display mode), `BB_FULLSCREEN=1` (desktop fullscreen when `BB_WINDOW_MODE` is unset), `BB_MUTE_UNFOCUSED=0`, `BB_OVERLAY_PAD=1` (overlay on `L3 + R3`), `BB_AUTO_DLC=0`, `BB_PREUPLOAD_FORCE=1`, `BB_STREAM_MEMORY=device|cached|host` (where the per-draw stream buffer lives: VRAM by default; `cached` / `host` put it in cached / uncached system memory, which were slower on an RTX 3070), `BB_SHADER_PRECOMPILE=0`, `BB_DRIVER_PIPELINE_CACHE=0` (the driver's pipeline binaries are not kept between sessions), `BB_ASYNC_SHADERS=0` / `BB_ASYNC_PIPELINES=0` (async compilation off; with `BB_ASYNC_SHADERS_POLICY=balanced|aggressive`, `BB_ASYNC_SHADERS_MAX_SKIP_FRAMES`, `BB_ASYNC_SHADERS_QUEUE`, `BB_SHADER_THREADS`, `BB_ASYNC_PIPELINE_THREADS` (background workers while playing: half the threads, 2 to 8, by default), `BB_ASYNC_PIPELINE_STREAK=N` (a pass may go without its new pipeline once its targets were drawn in each of the last N frames; 2 by default)), `BB_GPL=1` (`BB_GPL_STATS=1`), `BB_COMPILE_INDICATOR=0`, `BB_COMPILE_PROGRESS_TEST=1`, `BB_MOUSE_LOOK_CURVE=0` (mouse look without the dead zone compensation), `BB_CAM_PROBE=1` (test aid: camera yaw / pitch in the log every 100 ms, the stick still turning it), `BB_MOUSE_TRACE=1`, `BB_PROMPT_STYLE=auto|playstation|xbox|nintendo|keyboard`, `BB_BUTTON_PROMPTS=0` (the game's own prompt icons always), `BB_VIRTUAL_PAD=VID:PID` (test aid: an SDL virtual gamepad with that USB id that is never pressed, so a test run has that pad type; with `SDL_GAMECONTROLLER_IGNORE_DEVICES_EXCEPT=0xVID/0xPID` real pads stay out of the run), `BB_PAD_FILE` token `keyhold=NAME` (test aid: an SDL key name, `_` for a space, held on the keyboard while listed).
